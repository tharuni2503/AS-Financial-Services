Documents required for a home loan

The exact list depends on the lender, but most ask for the same core papers.

Everyone

  • Identity proof: Aadhaar, PAN, passport or voter ID
  • Address proof: Aadhaar, utility bill or rental agreement
  • Recent passport-size photographs and a filled application form

Salaried applicants

  • Latest 3 months’ salary slips and Form 16
  • Last 6 months’ salary-account bank statements

Self-employed applicants

  • Income tax returns with computation for the last 2–3 years
  • Profit & loss account, balance sheet and business proof
  • Last 6–12 months’ business bank statements

Property documents

  • Sale agreement or allotment letter, title deed and approved plan
  • Builder or seller details and payment receipts, where applicable

How your EMI is calculated

A simple way to understand what you pay each month and why.

Your EMI depends on three things: the loan amount (P), the monthly interest rate (r, which is the yearly rate divided by 12 and by 100) and the number of monthly instalments (n).

EMI = P × r × (1 + r)n ÷ ((1 + r)n − 1)

  • In the early years, most of each EMI goes towards interest. Over time, more goes towards principal.
  • A longer tenure lowers the EMI but raises the total interest you pay.
  • A part-payment reduces your outstanding principal, which cuts future interest.

How to improve your approval chances

Small steps before you apply can make a real difference.

  • Check your credit report. Many lenders prefer a higher credit score; fix errors and clear overdue amounts first.
  • Keep existing EMIs in check. Lenders look at how much of your income already goes to repayments. See the eligibility calculator.
  • Avoid applying to many lenders at once. Each hard enquiry can lower your score.
  • Add a co-applicant with stable income if it helps your eligibility.
  • Keep documents ready and consistent. Matching names, addresses and income figures avoid delays.

Approval is always at the lender’s discretion and subject to its policy.

Is a balance transfer worth it?

Moving your loan can save money, but only when the numbers work.

  • Rate gap: a small difference may not cover the costs of switching.
  • Remaining tenure: savings are larger when many years remain; late in the loan, most of the interest is already paid.
  • Fees: check processing fee, GST, legal charges and any foreclosure charges on your current loan.
  • Outstanding amount: larger balances benefit more from a lower rate.
